# Alabama Crimson Tide football

Curated reference for Tide AI. These are long-settled facts. For anything from the 2024 season onward (records, rosters, stats, results) use the live tools instead of this file.

## Program basics

- School: The University of Alabama, Tuscaloosa, Alabama.
- First season: 1892.
- Colors: crimson and white.
- Conference: Southeastern Conference (SEC), a charter member since the league began play in 1933. The SEC dropped its East/West divisions in 2024, when Texas and Oklahoma joined.
- Home stadium: Bryant–Denny Stadium (see Stadium).
- Mascot: Big Al, an elephant.
- Greeting and rallying cry: "Roll Tide."

## Where the names come from

- "Crimson Tide": usually credited to Hugh Roberts, a former sports editor of the Birmingham Age-Herald, describing the 1907 Iron Bowl, played in a sea of red mud.
- The elephant: in 1930 Atlanta Journal sportswriter Everett Strupper wrote about the "Red Elephants" after hearing fans react to the size of Wallace Wade's linemen. Big Al first appeared as a costumed mascot in the late 1970s.

- 2009: beat Texas 37–21 in the BCS title game at the Rose Bowl.

## College Football Playoff history (through 2023)

The four-team CFP ran from the 2014 to 2023 seasons; it expanded to 12 teams in 2024.

- 2014: lost Sugar Bowl semifinal to Ohio State, 42–35.
- 2015: beat Michigan State 38–0 (Cotton Bowl), then Clemson 45–40 for the title.
- 2016: beat Washington 24–7 (Peach Bowl), lost the title game to Clemson 35–31.
- 2017: beat Clemson 24–6 (Sugar Bowl), then Georgia 26–23 in overtime for the title.
- 2018: beat Oklahoma 45–34 (Orange Bowl), lost the title game to Clemson 44–16.
- 2019: missed the Playoff.
- 2020: beat Notre Dame 31–14 (Rose Bowl, played in Arlington, Texas), then Ohio State 52–24 for the title.
- 2021: beat Cincinnati 27–6 (Cotton Bowl), lost the title game to Georgia 33–18.
- 2022: missed the Playoff; beat Kansas State 45–20 in the Sugar Bowl.
- 2023: lost the Rose Bowl semifinal to Michigan 27–20 in overtime.

## Head coaches

Coaches who won national titles, plus the modern line of succession:

- Wallace Wade (1923–1930): three national titles. His 1925 team won the Rose Bowl on Jan. 1, 1926, beating Washington 20–19, the first Southern team to win it.
- Frank Thomas (1931–1946): titles in 1934 and 1941.
- Paul "Bear" Bryant (1958–1982): six national titles. Played end at Alabama in the 1930s. Retired after 1982 with 323 career wins, then the major-college record. His houndstooth hat is an enduring symbol of the program. Bryant died in January 1983, weeks after his last game.
- Ray Perkins (1983–1986), Bill Curry (1987–1989).
- Gene Stallings (1990–1996): 1992 national title with an unbeaten team.
- Mike DuBose (1997–2000), Dennis Franchione (2001–2002), Mike Shula (2003–2006).
- Nick Saban (2007–2023): six national titles at Alabama (2009, 2011, 2012, 2015, 2017, 2020), seven overall counting LSU's 2003 title. Retired in January 2024.
- Kalen DeBoer (2024–present): hired in January 2024 from Washington, which he had just led to the CFP title game.

## Stadium

- Bryant–Denny Stadium opened in 1929 as Denny Stadium, named for university president George H. Denny. Bear Bryant's name was added in 1975.
- Capacity is about 100,077 after the 2020 renovation, among the largest stadiums in the country.
- For decades Alabama also played some home games at Legion Field in Birmingham. Its home Iron Bowl games moved to Bryant–Denny in 2000.

## Traditions

- "Yea Alabama": the fight song, written in 1926 by student Ethelred Lundy "Epp" Sykes.
- "Rammer Jammer": the cheer the student section yells after a win ("…we just beat the hell out of you!").
- The Million Dollar Band: the marching band, a name from the 1920s.
- Walk of Champions: the brick plaza on the north side of Bryant–Denny with statues of the coaches who won national titles: Wade, Thomas, Bryant, Stallings and Saban.
- Tide Walk: the team walks through the crowd into the stadium before home games.
- Denny Chimes: the campus bell tower. Team captains since 1948 leave their handprints and cleat prints in the Walk of Fame around its base.
- Victory cigars: players and staff light cigars after beating Tennessee, a custom started by trainer Jim Goostree.
- Houndstooth: Bear Bryant's hat pattern, worn by fans everywhere.

## Rivalries

### Iron Bowl (Auburn)
- First played in 1893. The series stopped after 1907 over disputes about officials and expenses and resumed in 1948.
- Played at Legion Field in Birmingham through 1988. Auburn first hosted at Jordan-Hare Stadium in 1989, and Alabama's home games moved to Bryant–Denny in 2000; it now alternates between the two campuses.
- Famous editions:
  - 1981: Bryant's 315th win, 28–17, passing Amos Alonzo Stagg as the winningest major-college coach at the time.
  - 2010 "Camback": Auburn rallied from 24–0 down to win 28–27 in Tuscaloosa.
  - 2013 "Kick Six": with the score tied 28–28 and one second left, Alabama tried a 57-yard field goal; Auburn's Chris Davis caught the miss and returned it 109 yards for the winning touchdown.
  - 2021: Alabama won 24–22 in four overtimes at Auburn after Bryce Young led a 97-yard tying drive late in regulation.
  - 2023 "4th-and-31": trailing 24–20 with 43 seconds left, Jalen Milroe threw a 31-yard touchdown to Isaiah Bond on fourth-and-goal from the 31 to win 27–24 at Auburn.

### Third Saturday in October (Tennessee)
- One of the South's oldest rivalries, traditionally played in mid-October. Alabama celebrates wins with victory cigars.

### LSU
- An intense modern rivalry, especially during Nick Saban's tenure (he had coached LSU). The 2011 regular-season game, a 9–6 LSU win in overtime, was billed as the "Game of the Century"; Alabama won the rematch for the BCS title, 21–0.

### Georgia
- Frequent opponent in high-stakes games: SEC Championship wins in 2012 (32–28), 2018 (35–28, with Jalen Hurts rallying the team in relief of Tua Tagovailoa), 2021 (41–24) and 2023 (27–24); the 2017 CFP title game (Alabama 26–23 in OT) and the 2021 CFP title game (Georgia 33–18). In 2024 Alabama beat Georgia 41–34 in Tuscaloosa.

### Others
- Ole Miss, Mississippi State, Texas A&M and Arkansas were annual SEC West opponents until divisions ended in 2024.

## Famous games and plays

- 1926 Rose Bowl: 20–19 over Washington, putting Southern football on the national map.
- 1979 Sugar Bowl (Jan. 1, 1979): the "Goal Line Stand." Leading Penn State 14–7, Alabama stopped two plays at the 1-yard line, Barry Krauss making the fourth-down tackle. Alabama won the national title.
- 1993 Sugar Bowl: George Teague's strip of Miami's Lamar Thomas from behind, in a 34–13 win for the 1992 title.
- 2009 SEC Championship: beat No. 1 Florida and Tim Tebow 32–13.
- 2018 CFP title game (2017 season): freshman Tua Tagovailoa replaced Jalen Hurts at halftime; in overtime, on 2nd-and-26, he threw a 41-yard touchdown to DeVonta Smith to win 26–23.
